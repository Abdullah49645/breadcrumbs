A location-privacy check for anyone who posts photos

Every photo you post is a breadcrumb.

One photo of your street says “a big city.” Ten of them say which block. Breadcrumbs replays your public photos through an AI geolocator, shows the moment it finds your neighbourhood, and tells you exactly which photos to hold back.

No account. The demo runs entirely in your browser. Your own photos are analysed by a local server and never stored.

01

Replay your feed

Photos are added one at a time, in the order you posted them. Watch the AI's belief about where you live sharpen with each one.

02

Find the giveaways

Leave-one-out testing ranks which photos do the damage, and region-by-region blurring shows which part of each photo leaks.

03

Get a fix plan

A search finds the smallest set of changes that pushes the AI's guess out of your neighbourhood, then re-runs the model to prove it.

Single-photo tools miss the real risk.

Stripping GPS and blurring faces protects one image. But a stalker doesn't look at one image — they look at your feed. A snowy street, a bookstore, a playground: each is vague alone. Together they triangulate. Breadcrumbs measures that combined leak.

  • Multi-photo fusionproduct-of-experts over ~1.7 km cells, with the model's photo-density prior divided out
  • Measured, not guessedevery claim comes from re-running the model, not from asking it to explain itself
  • Defensive by designanalyses only photos you upload; no profile lookups, no scraping

Pick 2–24 photos you've posted publicly, in the order you posted them. GPS tags are stripped before analysis, so the AI sees only pixels.
